What is Isha?

23 November 2026

Isha is the night prayer, the last of the five daily prayers. It has four rak'ahs, is prayed after Maghrib, and its time ends at midnight, not at dawn.

Four rak'ahs, after Maghrib

Isha has four rak'ahs, or two for a traveller (see how many rak'ahs is each prayer). It comes after Maghrib, and the order matters: praying Isha before Maghrib on purpose makes it invalid, unless there is only time left for Isha, when Isha is prayed first (Ayatollah al-Sistani, ruling 723). If you pray Isha first by mistake and notice afterwards, it counts; pray Maghrib after it (ruling 724).

Many Shia pray Maghrib and Isha one after the other; Do Shia Muslims pray three times a day? explains why.

When Isha's time ends

Isha's time ends at midnight (rulings 723 and 726). This is Shari'i midnight, the middle of the night, not 12 o'clock on the clock (see what Shari'i midnight means). It moves with the length of the night: in London it is about 11:15 p.m. in late December and about 12:45 a.m. in late June; in Najaf it stays between about 11:20 and 11:40 p.m. all year.

If you miss it before midnight

If you could not pray before midnight because you forgot or overslept, for example, the time for Maghrib and Isha lasts until dawn (ruling 723). If you put it off on purpose, as an obligatory precaution pray it before the Fajr adhan, without intending it as on time or as qadha (ruling 727).

The Isha time in the app

Shia Namaz shows Isha when the evening twilight has faded, with the sun 14° below the horizon, under both the Qom and Tehran methods. That is about 50 minutes after Maghrib in Najaf and about an hour and ten minutes after it in London in December. The Midnight row beneath it shows when Isha's time ends. If your mosque uses another angle, you can set it under Custom.
