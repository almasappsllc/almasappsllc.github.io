How do Shia Muslims know when the new moon can be seen?

14 November 2026

By looking for it. A new Hijri month starts when the new crescent is actually seen after sunset. Before that, astronomers predict where and when it can be seen, so people know where to look. Under the rulings Shia Muslims follow, a prediction alone does not start the month.

What counts as seeing it

In Ayatollah al-Sistani's rulings, a month begins when the crescent is seen with the naked eye, by you, by two just witnesses, or by enough people that their reports give you confidence. Seeing it only through a telescope is not enough. If nobody sees it, the month runs to 30 days (ruling 1699). Astronomers' predictions do not start a month, unless they give you certainty or confidence (ruling 1701).

Most people follow their marja's office, or the centre that represents him locally. Other marja' differ on some points; why Eid can fall on different days explains how.

How astronomers predict it

The most widely used method is the Odeh criterion, published by the Jordanian astronomer Mohammad Odeh in the journal Experimental Astronomy. He built it from 737 crescent observations, almost half of them gathered by the Islamic Crescents' Observation Project (ICOP).

On each evening after the new moon, it looks at two things at sunset: how high the moon stands above the sun, and how wide the crescent is. Each place falls in one of four zones:

  • A: easily seen with the naked eye.
  • B: seen with binoculars or a telescope, and with the naked eye in perfect conditions.
  • C: seen only with binoculars or a telescope.
  • D: not seen, even with a telescope.

A map like this shows where a sighting is likely and which reports are believable. Clouds, haze and the observer's eyes still decide whether the moon is actually seen.

How the app shows it

Shia Namaz runs the Odeh criterion on your phone for your own location, with no internet. It starts each Hijri month after the first evening your location is in zone A, where the crescent should be easy to see with the naked eye. That is an estimate of a sighting, not a sighting, so it can be a day off the announcement you follow. In Settings, under Hijri calendar, you can move every Hijri date by up to three days to match it. This site shows the estimate, without that setting.
