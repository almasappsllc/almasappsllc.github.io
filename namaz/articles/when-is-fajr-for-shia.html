When is Fajr for Shia Muslims?

18 November 2026

Fajr starts at true dawn, when the first light spreads along the eastern horizon, and its time lasts until sunrise.

True dawn and false dawn

Before dawn, a pale band of light can rise upward from the east. This is the first dawn, also called the false dawn, and Fajr has not started yet. When the light spreads out along the horizon, that is the second dawn, the true dawn, and the time for Fajr begins. It ends when the sun rises (Ayatollah al-Sistani, ruling 728).

How a timetable works it out

Few people can watch the horizon every morning, so timetables calculate dawn from how far the sun is below the horizon. Shia Namaz offers the two standard Ja'fari settings:

  • Qom: the sun 16° below the horizon.
  • Tehran: the sun 17.7° below, so Fajr comes earlier, usually by about 8 to 18 minutes depending on the place and season.

Pray by the one your mosque or your marja's office uses. Around midsummer far from the equator, as in the UK, the sun never gets that far below the horizon, so the app uses a high-latitude rule instead.

Fajr and fasting

In Ramadan, the fast also starts at true dawn. Many timetables print an earlier time, Imsak, as a safety margin. What is Imsak? explains it.
