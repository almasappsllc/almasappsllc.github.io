What does Shari'i midnight mean?

On a prayer timetable, midnight can be a calculated point in the night, not the clock changing from 11:59 to 12:00.

About 3 minutes · Includes the Shia Namaz app's calculation definition

Midnight changes with the night

In a published answer, the Office of Ayatollah al-Sistani defines midnight as halfway between sunset and true dawn. The answer gives an example: sunset at 7 p.m. and true dawn at 4 a.m. gives a midpoint of 11:30 p.m. Because those events move by location and season, this midnight changes too. Read the full answer about finding midnight.

How Namaz calculates its row

Namaz calculates Shari'i midnight as the midpoint between its computed Maghrib and the following day's Fajr. It uses the chosen Qom or Tehran settings for those times. The row is therefore connected to the selected calculation method and the app's own definitions.

This is a software definition, not a universal ruling for every marja' or circumstance. The official answer above describes its own criteria. If you need to know which time applies to a particular prayer or situation, consult your marja' or local scholar.

Why it won't always say 12:00

Clock midnight is fixed by the civil time zone. The midpoint of sunset and dawn is determined by the length of the local night. In summer and winter, or when you travel, the two can be far apart. That's why a timetable may list a Shari'i midnight earlier or later than 12:00 a.m.

A timetable's “midnight” row helps make the calculation visible. It does not replace the guidance of your marja' for a ruling about when a prayer is due or delayed.
