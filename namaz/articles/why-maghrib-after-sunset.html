GUIDE 01 · PRAYER TIMES

Why is Shia Maghrib after sunset?

Sunset is an astronomical event. Maghrib is a prayer time. A timetable may show both because they are not always treated as the same moment.

About 3 minutes · Reviewed against Namaz's Qom and Tehran settings

Sunset is a marker, not the whole calculation

Sunset is when the sun drops below the horizon. In Ja'fari practice, the start of Maghrib is commonly tied to the evening twilight after sunset. The exact way that sign is identified can depend on the authority and method a person follows.

For example, Ayatollah Sistani's published ruling says to wait until the redness in the eastern sky that appears after sunset has passed overhead, based on obligatory precaution. See Islamic Laws, ruling 722. That ruling is one marja's guidance; it should not be presented as everyone's only method.

Why Namaz shows two rows

Namaz separates Sunset from Maghrib so the astronomical sunset remains visible as a reference while the selected Ja'fari method sets Maghrib later. Its Qom preset uses a 4° solar depression angle for Maghrib; the Tehran preset uses 4.5°. These are calculation settings in the app, not a claim that every scholar uses the same angle.

If your mosque or marja' gives a specific local timetable or calculation setting, follow that guidance. In Namaz, choose the matching method or enter custom angles when you know the values to use.

What the calculator can and cannot tell you

The calculator on this site runs the Shia Namaz app's own prayer engine. It calculates from your coordinates, date and chosen Qom or Tehran preset. It does not observe the sky, check a mosque's timetable or decide which religious ruling applies to you.

For a dependable daily practice, use the method recommended by your marja' or local religious authority. Treat a general online calculator as a tool for estimating and comparing times.
