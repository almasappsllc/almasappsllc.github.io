Why can a phone compass drift?

The direction can jump or slowly move even when you’re standing still. Often, the surroundings are affecting the sensor.

Nearby objects can interfere

Phone compasses use magnetic sensors. Magnets, speakers, metal furniture, a car dashboard, or other electronics can distort the local magnetic field. Move away from the object and check the reading again.

Try a figure-eight motion

If the compass app asks you to calibrate, follow its on-screen instructions. Many phones use a slow figure-eight movement to help the sensors separate Earth’s magnetic field from nearby interference. Recheck the reading outdoors and away from large metal objects.

Remember the north reference

A map bearing is measured from true north. A magnetic compass points toward magnetic north, and the difference between them varies by location. A phone may account for that difference, but sensor quality and calibration still affect its heading.
